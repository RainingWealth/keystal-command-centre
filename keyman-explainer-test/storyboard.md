# Storyboard / Treatment — "What Happens When Your Most Important Employee Disappears?"

Written before opening the Swiss demo's `DEMO.md` or code (library rule). Format 1080×1920, 24 fps, ≈52 s, 120 BPM (1 beat = 0.5 s = 12 frames, 1 bar = 2 s).

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **One grid, one subject (chosen).** A single 3×3 module grid is the "programme": it starts as a backup dashboard, becomes the business network around one person, loses that person, receives protection, recovers, and returns stable. One artifact, re-flowed five times. The person is the only cyan element until the shield arrives — the protagonist and, later, the thing being protected.
2. **Before / after split spread.** Left page "business with key person", right page "business without". Clear, but it states the problem once and then has nowhere to go in the Keyman and recovery beats; static compare.
3. **Countdown of consequences.** A running "days since the key person left" counter with a bar per consequence. Dramatic, but implies concrete numbers/timelines that cannot be substantiated and invites a claim-style reading.

Why 1: it gives every beat of the brief a spatial home (hook → network → deletion → protection → three-way split → stable return), keeps motion continuous across scenes (re-flow, not cuts), and the bookend (hook "?" → knife-cut → ✓) is built into the artifact.

## 2. Logline and arc
A backup dashboard shows everything safe — except the person the business can't restore from backup; remove them and the network ripples; Keyman insurance is shown as conditional financial protection that funds recruitment, expenses and time; the network ends stable and the "?" becomes a ✓.
Setup (0–6.25 s) → Turn (knife cut ≈10.8 s; hard silence 18–19 s) → Resolution (28.5–44 s) → CTA hold (44.25–52 s).

## 3. Benchmark
- **Learn:** Experimental Jetset / Pentagram identity-in-motion for snap-to-beat grids and clip-reveal type; Müller-Brockmann-era posters for scale contrast (giant numerals beside tiny notes) and flush-left hierarchy. Dieter Rams-style restraint for the icon set.
- **Don't take:** any specific poster composition, Helvetica/Akzidenz files, concentric-circle layouts, any Lemo-Opuscar demo story, shots, timeline or score.

## 4. Palette and grid (brief overrides the style's example colours)
Page `#0B1220`, panels `#17243A`, ink `#FFFFFF`, grid hairlines white 5–10 %. **Cyan `#00D9E8` = single signal colour** (key person, protection, CTA block). **Orange `#FF9A3C` = risk state, Scene 2 only**, never in the same element as cyan. One family: Archivo (OFL), weights 500–800, tracking −2 % ≥ 90 px. No gradients, shadows, glow, grain, bounce or fades.
Frame grid 1080×1920: 6 columns, 72 px margins, 24 px gutters. Module grid for the network: 3 × 3 cells of 292 × 210 px, 30/25 px gutters, y 500–1180. Zones: top band y 190–470 (headline), content y 200–1180, caption band y 1222–1480; right 72 px and bottom 440 px kept free of anything that must be read (platform UI).

## 8. Subtitle and title design
Captions are part of the layout: flush-left at column 1 (x = 72) in the caption band, Archivo SemiBold 54 px, line height 66 px, ≤ 2 lines, max width 828 px (clear of the right UI rail), a 2-px white-20 % rule above the band. Each word clip-reveals (rises from below the baseline) on its Whisper timestamp; the cue leaves upward. Long lines (> ≈46 characters) are split at phrase breaks into two or three chunks. Hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s) per line, asserted in `build_timeline.py`. Headlines: Archivo ExtraBold 88–120 px, −2 % tracking, uppercase, flush-left, words rise out of a clip rect.
