# THE PARTNER WHO NEVER CAME BACK: treatment

**Look:** written for the Paper-cut Lightbox style (Lemo-Opuscar library); the film was later also made as a photoreal cut with generated stills (see `style/STYLE.md`); both share this structure. Vertical 1080×1920 at 30 fps, ≤ 120 s. **Voice:** Kokoro (offline), British male/female voices cast per character. **Picture:** a stack of backlit cut-paper sheets with jointed silhouette figures; no lip-sync, performance comes from posture, light, framing and sound.

## 1. Three structures considered

1. **Straight line with a rewind pivot.** Hook on the hospital corridor, flashback to the friendship, the death, the funding crisis, then the film physically rewinds (the box pulls back, the earlier film plays backwards) and replays the same moment with a plan. Echo: the same meeting room, now warm and calm.
2. **Two parallel boxes.** Two lamps side by side, "unplanned" and "planned", running the same events at once. Clear for an explainer, but the viewer is taught before they feel anything.
3. **One window, one year.** A locked lamp, scene changes by season. Beautiful, too abstract for a financial-planning message.

**Chosen: 1.** The brief says the audience must feel the problem before being taught the solution. Structure 1 spends 75 s on the problem and uses the rewind (the lamp *as an object* that can be turned back) as the native move at the emotional peak.

## 2. Logline and arc

Two founders built a company together; one dies, and the survivor discovers that a company worth S$2 million does not mean S$1 million in cash. The film rewinds to show the same loss with an agreement and funding in place.

Setup (0:00–0:15) friendship and the buzzing phone → **Turn** (0:15–0:27) the call → Problem (0:27–1:14) estate, valuation ≠ cash, no funded exit → **Native move** (1:14–1:19) rewind → Solution (1:19–1:38) agree, fund, transfer → Ending (1:38–1:48) the same room, calm; the family and the business both continue → CTA (1:48–2:00).

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from:** the wordless life-montage-then-loss construction of the opening sequence of a well-known animated feature (compression of years, one long silence at the loss); Lotte Reiniger's jointed silhouette staging for gesture; commercial paper-cut shadow-box lamps for the material.
- **Do not take:** any character, composition, melody or shot from those works.

## 7. Titles and subtitles

Paper type (Cormorant, OFL) on cream strips that grow from an anchor edge. Spoken lines are captioned (burned in, warm off-white serif, soft shadow, in the social safe zone, with the speaker's name above dialogue); words that appear as paper are not captioned again. End card on navy with the disclosure. Texts held for reading time; the end card uses 20 characters/second instead of the default 15 so the film fits in 120 s (noted in the review).
